# Unit Economics: What Each User Costs to Serve

What it costs HomeGentic each month to support one user of each kind, what
that user pays, and how the totals move from 1,000 to 1,000,000 accounts.

This replaces the "Financial basis" section that used to live in
[AI_RATE_LIMITS.md](AI_RATE_LIMITS.md) (which predated photos on R2, the
Free tier's AI access, the 200/day chat cap, Stripe Identity, the weekly Home
Pulse email, SMS, signed records, the document vault and the other features
added since) and the on-chain-only figures in
[IMAGE_STORAGE_COST_ANALYSIS.md](IMAGE_STORAGE_COST_ANALYSIS.md).

**The numbers are a model, not measurements.** Nothing runs on mainnet yet, so
there is no production usage. Prices are list prices checked on the date in the
tables; usage is estimated per user type and bounded by the caps the code
enforces. Every input is in
[`scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs`](../scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs)
with its source. To change one, edit it and run
`node scripts/cost-model/costModel.mjs --write`; CI fails if the tables below
don't match the assumptions.

## Summary

- **AI is about three quarters of the bill** at every scale. Everything else
  (canisters, storage, email, Workers, relays) is small. Photos and documents
  live in Cloudflare R2, which costs about 1/30th of on-chain storage and has
  no egress fees, so storage is now a rounding error.
- **A typical Free homeowner costs about $0.72 a month** and brings in
  nothing. A typical **Pro homeowner costs about $2.42 a month against $4.92
  of revenue**: a thin but positive margin. A heavy Pro user (about 5 agent
  calls and 10 chats a day) runs at a loss, and one who maxes every daily cap
  could cost over $200 a month. The caps keep that rare, not impossible.
- **Contractor Pro ($40/mo) and realtors (the $399 Bid to List fee) are
  where the margin is.** Contractor Pro costs $2–15 a month to serve.
- **Pro subscriptions alone can't carry the Free tier at the assumed 10 Free
  users per Pro user.** The average Pro user covers about 3.5 average Free
  users. The model only makes money because of contractor and realtor revenue
  (about a third of revenue in the mix below), which is the least certain
  input.
- **Two cheap levers cut the AI bill by about a third:** prompt-caching the
  agent's 6,700-token tool list and system prompt (no route caches today), and
  moving `AI_MODEL` from Sonnet 4.6 to Sonnet 5.5, which lists at $2/$10
  instead of $3/$15.
- **The shared free AI budget runs out at about 1,600 typical Free users.**
  `FREE_AI_DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET` (10M weighted tokens, about $30 a day) is
  shared by every free account. Past that, free users get "budget reached"
  every day unless the budget grows, at roughly $0.55 per typical free user
  a month.

## Who the user types are

| Type | Pays | AI limits |
|---|---|---|
| Free homeowner | $0 | 10 agent calls/week, 3 chats/day, 25 other AI calls/day (3 in the first week); shares the free AI budget |
| Pro homeowner | $59/year | 10 agent calls/day, 200 chats/day, 200 other AI calls/day |
| Contractor Free | 3% of each winning bid, $20 minimum | no agent calls, 3 chats/day |
| Contractor Pro | $40/month (or $400/year) | as Pro |
| Realtor | $399 per Bid to List award, no subscription | Free limits |
| FSBO seller | $0 (a Free homeowner with an active listing) | Free limits |

There is no Premium tier any more: homeowner pricing collapsed to Free and Pro.

## How to read the tables

- **Usage levels.** *Light* is someone who signs up, adds a home and checks in
  occasionally. *Typical* is a weekly user who logs jobs, asks the assistant a
  few questions and uploads documents. *Heavy* is a daily power user (for Free,
  the agent and chat caps maxed). Pro also has an *every cap maxed* row: the
  cost if one account used every daily allowance every day, which is the abuse
  ceiling rather than a forecast.
- **Storage** is what a user has piled up after about two years (R2 photos and
  documents plus their records on-chain). Owner caps bound it: Free 100 photos
  / 150 MB, Pro 5,000 / 2 GB (`photo.ownerCapFor`).
- **Canister calls** are mostly threshold-key calls (about $0.035 each): the
  vetKD-encrypted score view, the Ed25519 signature on each report link and
  score certificate, and sealed-bid reveals. Ordinary update calls cost about
  $0.00003 each and queries are free.
- **Lookups + ID** are one-off costs spread over two years: a Stripe Identity
  check ($1.50) when an owner claims a home, the Google Places address lookup,
  ATTOM property details, a permit import and Rentcast prefill.
- **Stripe fees** include Stripe Billing's 0.7% on subscriptions. Revenue in
  the tables is before fees; fees are counted as cost.
- **Email** in the per-user rows uses the marginal Resend rate; the scale
  table charges the actual plan step. Every homeowner gets the weekly Home
  Pulse email unless they turn it off.
- **Scale** applies the user and usage mix shown above the table to every
  account. Real cohorts have many inactive accounts, which mostly cost nothing
  (light Free is about $0.13 a month, mostly the score view and one-off
  lookups).

## Fixed platform costs

At launch about $95 a month, nearly all of it tools, not infrastructure:
Cloudflare Workers Paid ($5, covering both Workers, Durable Objects, R2's free
allowance and Turnstile), four Fly.io machines for the notification relay and
IoT gateway in production and testnet (~$14 with volumes), ICP baseline cycles
for 2 × 21 canisters (~$5), Twilio number and 10DLC campaign (~$12), Expo EAS
($19), Sentry ($26), the Apple developer account ($8) and DNS. The model steps
this up past 50k accounts (larger relay machines), 100k (Sentry volume) and
500k (a datastore and second region for relay state). Not included: people,
support, marketing, legal, accounting, the ATTOM and OpenPermit contract
minimums, and canister cycles held as working capital (about 5T cycles, ~$7,
per canister before the freezing threshold).

## Where scale breaks before cost does

These are product or architecture limits that hit before the bill does. Each
one is cheap to raise in dollars but needs a deliberate change.

| Limit | Where | Binds at (model mix) | Raising it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free AI budget, 10M weighted tokens/day | `FREE_AI_DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET` | ~1,600 typical Free users | ~$0.55 per typical Free user a month |
| vetKD score views, 200/day platform-wide | `market` `MAX_DERIVES_PER_DAY_TOTAL` | ~5k accounts (the model has ~2.5 threshold calls per account a month, about half of them score views) | $0.035 per view |
| Signed report links + certificates, 500/day | `report` `MAX_ATTESTS_PER_DAY` | ~10–20k accounts (unsigned after that) | $0.035 per signature |
| Sealed-bid reveals, 500/day | `quote` `MAX_REVEALS_PER_DAY_TOTAL` | depends on quote volume | $0.035 per reveal |
| ATTOM 300/day, permits 1,000/day, address lookups 5,000/day, invite emails 500/day, DBPR 200 downloads/day | Worker vars | ATTOM first, at ~9,000 new homes a month (~200k accounts) | the vendor's per-call price |
| Weekly Home Pulse: one send every 600 ms, Monday–Tuesday window | notifications relay `pulse.ts` | ~250k homeowners (the week is skipped after that) | batch sends (Resend batch API) and more relay capacity |
| Relay state is one JSON file on one machine | notifications relay `persist.ts` | well before 1M accounts | a real datastore (D1, Postgres) |
| Per-canister upgrade ceilings (~2M jobs, ~1.2M photo records, ~6M sensor events) | upgrade-at-scale test | ~250k active homes after a year of jobs | sharding the job/photo/sensor canisters |

## Biggest cost drivers, in order

1. **Agent calls** (about $0.035 each). Each request resends 26,700 characters
   of tool definitions and the system prompt; prompt caching would bill most
   of that at a tenth of the price.
2. **Chat and document AI** (about $0.009 per chat, $0.025 per document).
   Pro's 200 chats and 200 AI calls a day mean a determined user can cost far
   more than they pay; watch the Worker's `agent_call` usage events after
   launch.
3. **Threshold-key calls** (about $0.035 each), mostly the encrypted score
   view. Caching the encrypted score per owner, or deriving it only when the
   score changes, would cut this.
4. **Stripe fees** (about 4% of a Pro year, 4.3% of a Contractor Pro month,
   3% of a $399 award).
5. **One-off verification** (Stripe Identity $1.50 per claimed home).

Everything else, including all on-chain storage and ordinary canister calls,
is under a cent per user per month.

## Things the model leaves out

- **The free Pro year for buyers.** A verified home handed over at closing
  gives the buyer a non-renewing Pro year (`payment.grantHandoffYear`): about
  $27 of serving cost with no revenue.
- **Agent credit packs** (25 or 100 extra agent calls, priced in Stripe) add
  revenue above the daily cap; their price isn't in the repo, so they're not
  modelled.
- **Refunds, disputes ($15 each) and failed renewals.**
- **Mobile store fees.** None: upgrades happen on the web (no in-app
  purchases), so Apple and Google take nothing.
- **Bid to List's own infrastructure**, which lives in its own repository.

## Changing the model

- Prices, token sizes, usage profiles, revenue assumptions, fixed costs and
  the user mix are all in `scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs`.
- `node scripts/cost-model/costModel.mjs` prints the tables;
  `--write` updates this file; `--check` (run in CI's lint job, with the
  tests in `costModel.test.mjs`) fails when the file is stale.
- When real usage exists, replace the profiles with measured means and
  percentiles from the Worker's usage events (`agent_call`, chat and aux
  counters), Anthropic's usage report, and the monitoring canister's
  per-method cycles.
