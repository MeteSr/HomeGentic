# Unit Economics: What Each User Costs to Serve

What it costs HomeGentic each month to support one user of each kind, what
that user pays, and how the totals move from 1,000 to 1,000,000 accounts.

This replaces the "Financial basis" section that used to live in
[AI_RATE_LIMITS.md](AI_RATE_LIMITS.md) (which predated photos on R2, the
Free tier's AI access, the 200/day chat cap, Stripe Identity, the weekly Home
Pulse email, SMS, signed records, the document vault and the other features
added since) and the on-chain-only figures in
[IMAGE_STORAGE_COST_ANALYSIS.md](IMAGE_STORAGE_COST_ANALYSIS.md).

**The numbers are a model, not measurements.** Nothing runs on mainnet yet, so
there is no production usage. Prices are list prices checked on the date in the
tables; usage is estimated per user type and bounded by the caps the code
enforces. Every input is in
[`scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs`](../scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs)
with its source. To change one, edit it and run
`node scripts/cost-model/costModel.mjs --write`; CI fails if the tables below
don't match the assumptions.

## Summary

- **AI is about two thirds of the bill** at every scale. Everything else
  (canisters, storage, email, Workers, relays) is small. Photos and documents
  live in Cloudflare R2, which costs about 1/30th of on-chain storage and has
  no egress fees, so storage is now a rounding error.
- **The assistant runs on Sonnet 5.5 with the tool list prompt-cached**
  (`AI_MODEL`, `anthropicProvider.requestBody`). That cut the total bill by
  about 30% against Sonnet 4.6 with no caching (see Sensitivity). The tool
  list is about 7,300 tokens, identical for every user, and is now billed at
  the cache-read rate.
- **A typical Free homeowner costs about $0.48 a month** and brings in
  nothing. A typical **Pro homeowner costs about $1.60 a month against $4.92
  of revenue**. A heavy Pro user (about 5 agent calls and 10 chats a day)
  still runs at a loss, and one who maxes every daily cap could cost about
  $170 a month. The caps keep that rare, not impossible.
- **Contractor Pro ($40/mo) and realtors (the $399 Bid to List fee) are
  where the margin is.** Contractor Pro costs $2–10 a month to serve.
- **Pro subscriptions alone still can't carry the Free tier at the assumed
  10 Free users per Pro user.** The average Pro user covers about 6.5 average
  Free users. The model only makes money because of contractor and realtor
  revenue (about a third of revenue in the mix below), which is the least
  certain input.
- **The shared free AI budget runs out at about 1,900 typical Free users.**
  `FREE_AI_DAILY_TOKEN_BUDGET` (10M weighted tokens, about $20 a day at Sonnet
  5.5 prices) is shared by every free account. Past that, free users get
  "budget reached" every day unless the budget grows, at roughly $0.32 per
  typical free user a month.
- **Questions asked through ChatGPT or Claude cost us almost nothing.** With
  the ICP MCP connector the user's own ChatGPT or Claude plan pays for the
  model, and we pay only for the canister calls (about $0.0001 a question
  against $0.007–0.013 in the app). If half of all users asked their
  questions there, the total bill would fall by about 20%; document and bill
  extraction stays in the app, so the saving per user tops out near half. See
  [ChatGPT and Claude connectors](#chatgpt-and-claude-connectors).
- **Token sizes need re-measuring.** They were measured for Sonnet 4.6;
  Sonnet 5.5's tokenizer counts the same text as more tokens (the model
  assumes 1.2×). Replace that factor with real numbers from the Worker's
  usage logs once traffic exists.

## How to read the tables

- **Usage levels.** *Light* is someone who signs up, adds a home and checks in
  occasionally. *Typical* is a weekly user who logs jobs, asks the assistant a
  few questions and uploads documents. *Heavy* is a daily power user (for Free,
  the agent and chat caps maxed). Pro also has an *every cap maxed* row: the
  cost if one account used every daily allowance every day, which is the abuse
  ceiling rather than a forecast.
- **Storage** is what a user has piled up after about two years (R2 photos and
  documents plus their records on-chain). Owner caps bound it: Free 100 photos
  / 150 MB, Pro 5,000 / 2 GB (`photo.ownerCapFor`).
- **Canister calls** are mostly threshold-key calls (about $0.035 each): the
  vetKD-encrypted score view, the Ed25519 signature on each report link and
  score certificate, and sealed-bid reveals. Ordinary update calls cost about
  $0.00003 each and queries are free.
- **Lookups + ID** are one-off costs spread over two years: a Stripe Identity
  check ($1.50) when an owner claims a home, the Google Places address lookup,
  ATTOM property details, a permit import and Rentcast prefill.
- **Stripe fees** include Stripe Billing's 0.7% on subscriptions. Revenue in
  the tables is before fees; fees are counted as cost.
- **Email** in the per-user rows uses the marginal Resend rate; the scale
  table charges the actual plan step. Every homeowner gets the weekly Home
  Pulse email unless they turn it off.
- **Scale** applies the user and usage mix shown above the table to every
  account. Real cohorts have many inactive accounts, which mostly cost nothing
  (light Free is about $0.12 a month, mostly the score view and one-off
  lookups).

## Fixed platform costs

At launch about $95 a month, nearly all of it tools, not infrastructure:
Cloudflare Workers Paid ($5, covering both Workers, Durable Objects, R2's free
allowance and Turnstile), four Fly.io machines for the notification relay and
IoT gateway in production and testnet (~$14 with volumes), ICP baseline cycles
for 2 × 21 canisters (~$5), Twilio number and 10DLC campaign (~$12), Expo EAS
($19), Sentry ($26), the Apple developer account ($8) and DNS. The model steps
this up past 50k accounts (larger relay machines), 100k (Sentry volume) and
500k (a datastore and second region for relay state). Not included: people,
support, marketing, legal, accounting, the ATTOM and OpenPermit contract
minimums, and canister cycles held as working capital (about 5T cycles, ~$7,
per canister before the freezing threshold).

## ChatGPT and Claude connectors

Since PR #706, HomeGentic publishes `/.well-known/ic-architecture`, which lets
DFINITY's ICP MCP connector (the "Chat with your ICP apps" app in ChatGPT, or
`https://mcp.internetcomputer.org/mcp` added as a custom connector in Claude)
call eight canisters for a signed-in user: property, job, maintenance,
recurring, bills, report, contractor and quote
(`frontend/scripts/icArchitecture.mjs`). It only works once those canisters
are on mainnet. What that means for cost, checked against the code:

- **The model is not ours.** The connector runs inside the user's ChatGPT or
  Claude conversation, so their subscription pays for every token. Nothing
  passes through the voice Worker: none of the eight canisters makes an HTTPS
  outcall or calls the Worker or Claude, so a connector question never touches
  `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, the free AI budget or the agent limits.
- **What we do pay for is canister calls.** `getApiDoc`, `candid:service` and
  most reads are queries, which the IC doesn't charge for today. But the reads
  that check access through the property canister are update calls
  (`job.getJobsForProperty`, `bills.getBillsForProperty`,
  `recurring.getByProperty`, `maintenance.getScheduleByProperty`,
  `report.getReport` and others), and so are writes. The model assumes 3
  update calls a question, about $0.0001.
- **Two exposed calls use threshold keys** (about $0.035 each): signing a
  report link or certificate (`report`, 500 a day platform-wide) and
  sealed-bid reveals (`quote`, 5 per request a day, 500 a day in total). The
  caps hold whether the call comes from the app or a connector.
- **The other AI stays in the app.** Document and bill extraction, photos and
  the rest of the Worker's features can't be reached through the connector,
  so the model leaves aux AI where it was. That's why the saving per user
  stops at about half even when every question moves.

The model's connector scenario moves a share of users' chat and agent calls to
the connector and assumes they ask 1.5× as many questions there, since it
costs them nothing extra (`CONNECTOR` in `assumptions.mjs`). Results are in
the generated tables above.

**Risks.**

- **Tool-call spam.** An assistant can loop on reads. Every exposed canister
  except `bills` caps update calls at 30 a minute per principal, so one
  account maxing all seven limits all day costs about $8 a day in cycles.
  `bills` has no per-minute limit at all. This isn't new exposure: the
  canisters were always callable directly by anyone with an agent library,
  and `Inspect.mo` already refuses anonymous callers and oversized
  arguments. But principals are free to create (the root cause in the
  2026-10-31 cycle-drain audit), so a script with many accounts multiplies
  the per-principal ceiling. Adding the shared `RateLimit` to `bills` closes
  the open one.
- **Fewer Pro upgrades.** The agent limits (10 a week on Free, 10 a day on
  Pro) are part of why people pay. A Free user with ChatGPT or Claude gets
  unlimited questions about their own records without hitting them. The model
  doesn't change revenue in the connector scenario; if connectors catch on,
  watch Free-to-Pro conversion.
- **Query charging.** If the IC starts charging for queries, every connector
  read starts to cost cycles too. Queries cost far less than updates, so the
  connector would still be much cheaper than in-app AI.
- **Usage we can't see.** Connector calls don't go through the Worker, so
  they won't show up in its usage events. Watch them in canister cycle burn
  (the monitoring canister and the hourly ops check).

## Biggest cost drivers, in order

1. **Document and chat AI** (about $0.020 per document, $0.007 per chat).
   Pro's 200 chats and 200 AI calls a day mean a determined user can cost far
   more than they pay; watch the Worker's `agent_call` usage events after
   launch.
2. **Agent calls** (about $0.013 each with the tool list cached; $0.035
   before). Each tool-use turn is a separate request.
3. **Threshold-key calls** (about $0.035 each), mostly the encrypted score
   view. Caching the encrypted score per owner, or deriving it only when the
   score changes, would cut this.
4. **Stripe fees** (about 4% of a Pro year, 4.3% of a Contractor Pro month,
   3% of a $399 award).
5. **One-off verification** (Stripe Identity $1.50 per claimed home).

Everything else, including all on-chain storage and ordinary canister calls,
is under a cent per user per month.

## Things the model leaves out

- **The free Pro year for buyers.** A verified home handed over at closing
  gives the buyer a non-renewing Pro year (`payment.grantHandoffYear`): about
  $27 of serving cost with no revenue.
- **Agent credit packs** (25 or 100 extra agent calls, priced in Stripe) add
  revenue above the daily cap; their price isn't in the repo, so they're not
  modelled.
- **Refunds, disputes ($15 each) and failed renewals.**
- **Mobile store fees.** None: upgrades happen on the web (no in-app
  purchases), so Apple and Google take nothing.
- **Bid to List's own infrastructure**, which lives in its own repository.

## Changing the model

- Prices, token sizes, usage profiles, revenue assumptions, fixed costs and
  the user mix are all in `scripts/cost-model/assumptions.mjs`.
- `node scripts/cost-model/costModel.mjs` prints the tables;
  `--write` updates this file; `--check` (run in CI's lint job, with the
  tests in `costModel.test.mjs`) fails when the file is stale.
- When real usage exists, replace the profiles with measured means and
  percentiles from the Worker's usage events (`agent_call`, chat and aux
  counters), Anthropic's usage report, and the monitoring canister's
  per-method cycles.
